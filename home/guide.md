## The principle

Cost-effective first. Change the cheapest part of a setup before replacing
hardware, buy one unit and live with it before buying for every room, and
put recurring upkeep on a calendar from day one.

## 1. Fragrance-free soap in every dispenser

**Status:** trial in progress.

**Goal:** every sink in the home has a gentle, fragrance-free hand soap, in a
dispenser that is easy to keep clean.

### Steps

| Step | Do this | Why now |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Switch the soap first, in the dispensers you already have | The soap is the part that touches skin, and it is the cheapest change |
| 2 | Pick a soap labelled **fragrance-free**, not "unscented" | "Unscented" products can contain masking fragrance |
| 3 | If anyone has sensitive or eczema-prone skin, check for the National Eczema Association Seal of Acceptance | The Seal means the ingredients were reviewed for that use |
| 4 | Buy **one** new dispenser and put it at the busiest sink | It gets the most use, so a problem shows up fastest |
| 5 | Run it for a few weeks with your chosen soap | Whether a particular soap dispenses well in a particular pump only shows in use |
| 6 | Only then replace the rest, or switch to the other option | A bad pick costs one unit, not a houseful |

### What to buy

| Tier | Option | Price | Power | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Budget | Keep your current dispensers; change only the soap | Soap only | None | Often enough on its own |
| Mid | Secura 17 oz Automatic | ~$24-29 | 4 × AA | Large tank, so fewer refills |
| Upgrade | simplehuman 9 oz Liquid Sensor Pump | $70-80 | Rechargeable, about 4 months per charge | 2-year warranty; smaller tank |

**Soap used in the trial:** Cetaphil Gentle Skin Cleanser. Its maker lists it
as accepted by the National Eczema Association. Confirm on the NEA's own
product directory before buying in bulk, since acceptance can change.

### Keeping dispensers clean

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Never top off a part-full dispenser. Empty it, wash it, dry it, then refill. | Topping off can lead to bacterial contamination of the soap |
| Write the refill date on the dispenser | Makes "how old is this soap?" answerable |

### When to stop and get help

If your skin turns red, sore or inflamed after switching soap, stop using the
new soap. See a doctor if the reaction is severe, does not clear up, or shows
signs of infection such as tenderness, warmth or fever.

## 2. Hot tub upkeep on a fixed schedule

**Status:** in use.

**Goal:** a hot tub that stays clean and balanced without having to remember
when anything was last done.

### Steps

| Step | Do this | Why now |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Write down the install date (or the date you took it over) | Every schedule below counts from it |
| 2 | Set up recurring calendar events for each task in the table below, on day one | Upkeep that lives in your head gets skipped |
| 3 | Buy the test strips your sanitizer system specifies | Some systems read wrong on standard test kits (see below) |
| 4 | Balance the water before relying on the sanitizer | A sanitizer works properly only in balanced water |

### Schedule

These intervals come from one tub maker (Caldera) and one sanitizer system
(FROG @ease). Your manuals win where they differ.

| How often | Task | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly | Clean the skimmer basket | Caldera |
| As your sanitizer's directions say | Test the water | Caldera; FROG |
| About every 3-4 weeks | Replace the FROG @ease SmartChlor cartridge (for an average 400 gal tub; varies with size and use) | FROG |
| Monthly | Rinse the filter with a garden hose | Caldera |
| Monthly, or at each SmartChlor change | Shock with a non-chlorine shock | FROG |
| Every 3-4 months | Drain and refill (chlorine or bromine systems) | Caldera |
| Every 4 months | Replace the FROG @ease mineral cartridge | FROG |
| Every 3 years | Replace the filter | Caldera |

### Water balance (FROG @ease)

| Reading | Target range |
|---|---|
| pH | 7.2-7.8 |
| Total alkalinity | 80-120 ppm |
| Hardness | 150-250 ppm |

Adjust total alkalinity first. Add one balancing chemical at a time, with the
jets on, and wait 6 hours before testing again.

### Choosing a test kit

Match the kit to the sanitizer. FROG @ease builds a chlorine reserve that
standard strips read as total chlorine, so its manual says to use FROG @ease
strips. A basic 3-way kit such as the Taylor K-1000 measures total chlorine,
bromine and pH only, not alkalinity, so it cannot cover the full balance table
above on its own.

### When to stop and get help

Electrical faults, error codes, leaks or water that will not clear after
shocking and rebalancing: call the dealer or a technician.
