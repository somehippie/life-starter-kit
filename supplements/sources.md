## Regulation and safety

| Claim | Source | Last verified | Checked |
|---|---|---|---|
| FDA cannot approve supplements before they are marketed; firms generally need not provide safety evidence to FDA; firms are responsible for products not being adulterated or misbranded | [FDA, Questions and Answers on Dietary Supplements](https://www.fda.gov/food/information-consumers-using-dietary-supplements/questions-and-answers-dietary-supplements) | 2026-10-02 | read at source |
| No law or regulation limits serving size or the amount of an ingredient per serving | FDA, same page | 2026-10-02 | read at source |
| Some products marketed for weight loss, sexual enhancement and bodybuilding contain prescription drugs or unlisted ingredients; FDA can't test all products | [NCCIH, Using Dietary Supplements Wisely](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/using-dietary-supplements-wisely) | 2026-10-02 | read at source |
| 776 adulterated supplements identified by FDA warnings 2007 through 2016, mostly sexual enhancement (45.5%), weight loss (40.9%) and muscle building (11.9%) | Tucker J, et al. [Unapproved Pharmaceutical Ingredients Included in Dietary Supplements Associated With US FDA Warnings](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30646238/). JAMA Netw Open. 2018;1(6):e183337 (abstract) | 2026-10-02 | read at source (abstract) |
| A maker's use of "standardized", "verified" or "certified" does not necessarily guarantee quality or consistency | NCCIH, Using Dietary Supplements Wisely | 2026-10-02 | read at source |
| "Natural" doesn't always mean safe; some botanicals can harm the liver | [NIH ODS, Dietary Supplements: What You Need to Know](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/WYNTK-Consumer/) | 2026-10-02 | read at source |
| Tell health care providers, including pharmacists, about supplements you take | NIH ODS, same page | 2026-10-02 | read at source |
