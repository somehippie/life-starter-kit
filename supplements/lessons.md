# Supplements: lessons

These are lessons about evaluating and keeping up with supplements, not about
what to take.

| Lesson | Why it tends to hold | How to apply it |
|---|---|---|
| An all-in-one product can quietly undo what you were careful about | Multivitamins and blends combine many ingredients, including ones that can interact; confirm with a pharmacist or doctor | Audit every ingredient in a combination product as if you were buying each one separately, then check the whole list with a pharmacist |
| Read the actual label, not the nutrient's reputation | General knowledge about a nutrient may not match what a specific product contains | When something seems off, read that exact product's full label, including "other ingredients", before drawing conclusions, and tell your doctor |
| Re-read the label when you reorder | Formulas, forms and serving sizes change between batches and sizes | Compare the new label with the old one before you start the new bottle |
| Fewer products are easier to keep consistent | Every extra product is another thing to remember, reorder and check | If you are not taking something regularly, ask whether it still earns its place |
| A routine sticks when it rides on an existing habit | Remembering a new standalone task is harder than adding to one you already do | Attach it to something you already do daily, after checking with a pharmacist that the pairing has no interaction |
| Buying from one brand simplifies reordering but not checking | Brands vary in quality from product to product | Audit each product on its own label and seal, even within a brand you trust |
| Write down why you take each item | Reasons get forgotten, and products pile up | Keep a short list: product, reason, who advised it, date started. Bring it to appointments. |

## What did not work

| Tried | What went wrong | Do this instead |
|---|---|---|
| Assuming an all-in-one product would simplify things | It combined ingredients that had deliberately been kept apart | Audit combination products ingredient by ingredient before switching |
| Explaining a reaction from a nutrient's general reputation | The specific product did not contain that nutrient at all; another ingredient was a more likely cause | Check the exact label first, then ask a doctor |
| Keeping a product that was only taken on some days | It was never taken consistently enough to judge | Decide whether it is needed; if not, stop buying it |
